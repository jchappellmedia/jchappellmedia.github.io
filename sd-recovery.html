Lost something just now? Stop using that card. Don't record on it, don't format it, don't keep opening it. The footage survives until something new is written over it.

What it does

  • Finds clips you deleted in-camera or lost to a quick format, even if you've recorded more since.
  • Tells you what's actually recoverable before you spend any time on it. It lists every lost clip with its recording date, length, size, and whether it's still intact.
  • Handles Sony's unusual file layout (FX3, FX30, A7 IV, A7S III, ZV-E1 and more), plus most other cameras that record MP4/MOV: Canon, Panasonic, Fujifilm, GoPro, DJI.
  • Is read-only by design. It can't damage your card, and it refuses to save onto the card it's reading.

How it works, in four steps

  1. Unmount the card so your computer stops writing to it.
  2. Scan. One command searches the card's free space and lists every lost clip it finds.
  3. Pick yours by date and length. It shows INTACT or DAMAGED next to each one.
  4. Recover it to your computer or an external drive. Done.

The download includes a plain-English README that walks through every command. If you've never opened Terminal before, you'll be fine.

What you need

  • A Mac or Linux computer (Windows isn't supported yet)
  • Python 3, already on most Macs
  • Free space on your computer or an external drive at least as big as the clip. Thirty minutes of 4K is about 25 GB.
  • An exFAT card. That's most cards over 32 GB, which covers nearly every card used for video.

What it can't do

If a clip has been fully recorded over, or the card was wiped with a full or low-level format, the footage is gone, and no software can bring it back. Cards 32 GB and under that use FAT32 aren't supported yet.
